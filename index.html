Learn Geospatial Data with Phoebe

A location is a claim with a reference system, a boundary choice and a distance formula behind it, and each of the three can be wrong silently. Six sessions on catching them: naming the system a coordinate lives in, deciding inside from outside by counting crossings, measuring a distance three ways and saying its error at your latitude, joining points to polygons without the full loop, reading a disease map against three denominators, and a bench that computes all of it live on real Singapore open data: 332 URA subzones, 613 MRT station exits, the NEA dengue clusters of one day and the Census 2020 population, through a constructed town-council data desk called Rivermont.

The six sessions 📍 · from "where is this row" to a bench that can fail

The running case is Rivermont, a constructed town-council data desk: Mei runs it, Arif brings the questions, Priya signs off. Rivermont and its people are invented for teaching, and every page says so. Everything the desk works on is real: Singapore open data from data.gov.sg, accessed 2026-10-22 under the Singapore Open Data Licence v1.0, with the licence line on every widget that shows it.

📍 Session 1 · foundations

Where is this row?

Two numbers are not a place until the reference system is named. WGS 84 degrees against SVY21 metres, what a degree is worth at 1 deg N, and the axis-order swap that a range check catches at row 1.

▶ Start here 🔷 Session 2 · core

Points, polygons and the subzone

332 URA subzones as rings and holes, the even-odd rule by hand and in code, what simplifying a boundary does to a count, and why the boundary you pick is a decision.

inside or outside 📏 Session 3 · core

Distance three ways

Raw degrees, haversine, projected metres. Why one constant almost works in Singapore and fails by two at 60 deg N, drawn as the cosine it is, and why the sphere is the bigger error near the equator.

cos(latitude) 🔲 Session 4 · deeper

Spatial joins and the grid index

613 points into 332 polygons without the full loop: bounding boxes first, then a 500 m grid-cell index from scratch. Same answers, 2,396 exact tests instead of 203,516.

candidate, then exact 🗺️ Session 5 · deeper

Disease on the map

NEA dengue clusters joined to subzones and planning areas, cases against three denominators, the choropleth that lies, and why COVID is not on the page. Geometry and joins, no health advice.

three denominators ⚖️ Session 6 · bench night

The distance-and-join bench

The whole ladder computed live: five rungs, 203,516 pairs, point in polygon three ways, an anti-lever that moves the points to 60 deg N and a break button that swaps the columns.
