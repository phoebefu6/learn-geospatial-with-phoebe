Part 1 · covers what an NEA cluster is and how it joins

A cluster is a locality, not an address

5 min live

Rivermont is constructed; the file Mei opens is real. NEA publishes the dengue clusters as polygons, and the definition on NEA's own page is the thing to read before any geometry: a cluster forms when "two or more cases have onset within 14 days and are located within 150m of each other", and "operationally, a dengue cluster indicates a locality with active transmission where intervention is targeted". Case locations are based on "residential and workplace addresses". So each polygon is a locality drawn around addresses, not an address, and the count on it is cases in that locality. To put it on Arif's map of subzones, Mei needs one more rule: which subzone does a polygon that may straddle a boundary belong to? Tonight's answer is the simplest honest one, the centroid, and the page says every place that choice bites.

LiveThe polygon, the count and the three text columns the page calls numeric3 min▶

The NEA dataset is d_dbfabf16158d1b0e1c420627c0819168, Dengue Clusters (GEOJSON). It changes daily; "map data is updated at 1am", and the snapshot on this page was downloaded on 2026-10-22, when the file was last updated at 10:06 SGT. Each feature carries LOCALITY, CASE_SIZE, NAME, HYPERLINK, HOMES, PUBLIC_PLACES, CONSTRUCTION_SITES and the usual INC_CRC and FMEL_UPD_D. CASE_SIZE is the count on the polygon. The dataset page types HOMES, PUBLIC_PLACES and CONSTRUCTION_SITES as Numeric; in the file they are text lists of breeding-site types, or null. That mismatch is worth a line in the notes before anyone tries to sum them.

Eight features on 2026-10-22, case sizes 96, 16, 13, 5, 4, 3, 2 and 2, total 141. NEA's alert levels are quoted as written: red is a "High-risk area with 10 or more cases", yellow a "High-risk area with less than 10 cases", green "No new cases, under surveillance for the next 21 days". Those are NEA's categories for NEA's purposes. The page uses the case count only as a number to join and divide; what a count means for anyone living there is NEA's and MOH's to say, at nea.gov.sg, dengue clusters.

Self-studyCentroid join: why it is simplest, and the two places it bites4 min▶

Session 2's centroid is the area-weighted planar centroid of the outer rings minus holes, in degrees, which is fine at 1 deg N. Session 4's point-in-polygon test puts that point in a subzone. The result is a many-to-one join: each cluster lands in exactly one subzone and, through the subzone's PLN_AREA_N attribute, exactly one planning area. On the 2026-10-22 snapshot the eight centroids fall in six subzones across five planning areas: two clusters in Jurong West (Taman Jurong and Kian Teck), one each in Outram (Chinatown), Bishan (Bishan East) and Serangoon (Upper Paya Lebar), and three in the single subzone of Tagore in Ang Mo Kio.

It bites in two ways. First, a polygon that straddles a boundary gives all of its cases to whichever side the centroid lands on; a cluster of 96 cases is treated the same as a cluster of 2. The alternatives, splitting cases by the share of area on each side or by the share of residents, are better in some ways and introduce assumptions of their own; which one a desk picks should be written down next to the table it produces. Second, a concave or multi-part locality can have a centroid that lies outside itself, exactly as session 2 showed for subzones. On this snapshot none of the eight does, and the build-along prints the check so you can see it fail the day it does.

Real world

The column typed Numeric that holds words. A familiar data-desk pattern: a catalogue page says a field is a number, a pipeline casts it, and the first row with text in it throws, or worse, becomes null quietly and the column sums to something small and plausible. The fix is never to trust the type on the page over the type in the file. Print the Python type of the first few values of every column before any arithmetic; a catalogue record is a claim about the data, and the data is the data.

Part 2 · covers three denominators, three maps

The choropleth that lies

6 min live

Rivermont is constructed; every number in this Part is from the real snapshot and Census 2020. Arif wants one map coloured from pale to dark. Mei has three honest numbers to colour it by. Cases per cluster: Taman Jurong has 96 and is the darkest thing on the island. Cases per 100,000 residents of the subzone, with the Census 2020 resident count as the denominator: Kian Teck has 2 cases and 40 residents, which is 5,000 per 100,000, twenty times Taman Jurong's 240.5, and now Kian Teck is the darkest cell and Taman Jurong is a mid-tone next door. Cases per 100,000 of the planning area: Jurong West, which holds both, is 98 cases over 262,730 residents, 37.3, and the whole west is one even shade. Nothing in the data moved. The denominator did.

LiveRate, denominator and the 40 residents of Kian Teck3 min▶

A rate is a count divided by a population, and the population has to be the population of the same shape as the count. Census 2020 gives resident population by planning area and by subzone, so there is a denominator for both levels: 332 subzone rows whose names match the URA SUBZONE_N field exactly once upper-cased, and 55 planning-area "Total" rows, one of them spelled "Changi- Total" without the space. 46 subzones carry "-" instead of a number. The Census is a count of residents at their place of residence; Kian Teck's 40 is a real figure for a subzone that is mostly not housing. Two cases in a locality whose polygon sits in Kian Teck, divided by 40 residents, gives 5,000 per 100,000, the largest rate on the island by a factor of twenty, from the smallest cluster in the file.

That is not an error in the arithmetic. It is a mismatch of shapes: NEA builds a locality from residential and workplace addresses, and the Census counts residents only, so the people the cases are drawn from and the people in the denominator are different sets. "Cases per resident of the subzone" is itself a boundary choice, and the figure that looks like a finding is the figure that most needs the sentence under it saying so.

Self-studySuppressed is not zero: the 46 subzones with no count3 min▶

The Census subzone table carries "-" for 46 of the 332 subzones. The dataset does not say, row by row, whether that means no residents or a figure withheld, so the honest reading is "suppressed or zero is not zero": ship it as null, never as 0. The reason is arithmetic. A zero denominator turns any cluster that lands there into a division error; a small denominator typed in by hand turns it into a rate larger than Kian Teck's. Null keeps the subzone on the map, with a hatched or empty cell and a legend entry that says "no resident count", which is the true state of knowledge. On the 2026-10-22 snapshot none of the eight centroids falls in one of the 46, so the three tables are complete; the code keeps the branch anyway, because the file changes daily and a cluster in an industrial subzone is exactly the case this branch exists for.

The second half of this lesson is the planning-area view. Aggregating up smooths everything: Jurong West's 37.3 hides a 5,000 and a 240.5 in one shade. That is the modifiable areal unit problem (Openshaw 1984, reported), and the depth of it, with sensor placement and what aggregation does for privacy, is taught next door: Smart City, zones, joins and the map and aggregation and privacy. How to colour a choropleth so the legend carries the denominator is Dataviz's: colour that works and choosing the form.

Real world

Priya's one question (constructed). Mei brings Priya the three maps. Priya does not ask which is right. She asks what sentence goes under each one, and whether Arif's team can act on the shape that is coloured. A planning area is too big to send anyone to; a subzone with 40 residents and a rate of 5,000 is a number about a denominator, not a place to go. The map that gets printed is the first one, cases per cluster, with the cluster polygons drawn as polygons and NEA's page linked for what the colours mean. The other two go in the appendix with their denominators written out. That is the desk's rule: a coloured map is never shipped without the division written under it.

Part 3 · covers why COVID is not on the page

The dataset that is not there

4 min live

Rivermont is constructed; the absence described here is real and is taught as reported. Arif's first draft of the question was about COVID, not dengue, because that is the disease everyone has a map of in their head. Mei looked for the data and found three things, each of which is a reason the page uses dengue clusters instead, and each of which is a general lesson about what makes a disease dataset usable for geometry at all: a location field, a licence, and a shape that is a locality rather than a person.

LiveThree things a disease dataset needs before it can be joined3 min▶

Reported, as stated in the approved brief of 2026-10-22 and to be re-verified at source by anyone who repeats it: MOH's open COVID datasets carry dates and counts and no location field, so there is nothing to join to a subzone; the 2020 map of places visited by cases was a visualisation rather than a downloadable dataset, so there is a picture and no rows; and the third-party scrapes that circulated are case-level data with no licence, so even where rows exist, nobody has the right to publish a join built on them. Each is a separate failure. The first is a schema problem, the second a publishing problem, the third a licensing problem, and a dataset has to pass all three before a desk can draw it.

Dengue clusters pass all three. They are open, under the Singapore Open Data Licence v1.0; they carry geometry; and the geometry is a polygon by design, a locality built from residential and workplace addresses with the addresses themselves never published. That last property is the one worth copying into any desk's own publishing. The privacy side of aggregation is taught in Smart City (aggregation and privacy, publishing open data); the law behind it is PDPA here and GDPR elsewhere.

Self-studyA snapshot is one day: what the daily file does to a time series2 min▶

The NEA file is overwritten daily and the dataset page does not serve history. Everything on this page is the 2026-10-22 download, and a learner who downloads tomorrow will see different clusters, different counts and possibly different subzones; the build-along prints "your numbers will differ" wherever that matters. A desk that wants a series has to keep its own dated copies, one file per day, with the access date in the licence notice of each. Gaps, overwrites and the clock behind a daily file are their own subject, taught in IoT data, clocks, sampling and resampling.

Write the absence down. "No location field in the MOH open tables, checked on this date" is a finding, and it belongs in the notes with the same care as a number. The next person to be asked for a COVID map will otherwise spend the same afternoon looking.

Centroid and point-in-polygon, from sessions 2 and 4. Area-weighted planar centroid over every ring of every polygon in a feature, holes subtracted, in degrees; even-odd ray casting over every ring so holes need no special case. Both are copied in, not rewritten.

★ Step 2 · the two functionsdef rings(geom): # every ring, with +1 for outer and -1 for holes polys = [geom["coordinates"]] if geom["type"] == "Polygon" else geom["coordinates"] for poly in polys: for i, ring in enumerate(poly): yield ring, (1 if i == 0 else -1) def centroid(geom): # area-weighted, outer rings minus holes A = Cx = Cy = 0.0 for ring, sgn in rings(geom): for (x1, y1), (x2, y2) in zip(ring, ring[1:] + ring[:1]): cross = x1 * y2 - x2 * y1 A += sgn * cross; Cx += sgn * (x1 + x2) * cross; Cy += sgn * (y1 + y2) * cross return Cx / (3 * A), Cy / (3 * A) def inside(x, y, geom): # even-odd over every ring hit = False for ring, _ in rings(geom): for (x1, y1), (x2, y2) in zip(ring, ring[1:] + ring[:1]): if (y1 > y) != (y2 > y) and x < x1 + (y - y1) * (x2 - x1) / (y2 - y1): hit = not hit return hit

Join each cluster to one subzone, and check the centroid is inside its own polygon. The second check is the one from session 2: a concave locality can have a centroid outside itself. On this snapshot all eight pass; the branch stays, because tomorrow's file is a different file.

★ Step 3 · the joindef strip(coords): # drop any altitude value so pairs unpack cleanly return [[[c[0], c[1]] for c in ring] for ring in coords] for f in sz["features"] + cl["features"]: g = f["geometry"] g["coordinates"] = strip(g["coordinates"]) if g["type"] == "Polygon" else [strip(p) for p in g["coordinates"]] joined = [] for c in cl["features"]: x, y = centroid(c["geometry"]) own = inside(x, y, c["geometry"]) home = [s["properties"] for s in sz["features"] if inside(x, y, s["geometry"])] assert len(home) == 1, ("a centroid in 0 or 2 subzones", c["properties"]["LOCALITY"]) joined.append((home[0]["SUBZONE_N"], home[0]["PLN_AREA_N"], int(c["properties"]["CASE_SIZE"]), own)) for row in sorted(joined, key=lambda r: -r[2]): print(f"{row[0]:<18} {row[1]:<12} {row[2]:>4} centroid inside own polygon: {row[3]}") # TAMAN JURONG JURONG WEST 96 centroid inside own polygon: True # BISHAN EAST BISHAN 16 centroid inside own polygon: True # TAGORE ANG MO KIO 13 centroid inside own polygon: True # CHINATOWN OUTRAM 5 centroid inside own polygon: True # UPPER PAYA LEBAR SERANGOON 4 centroid inside own polygon: True # TAGORE ANG MO KIO 3 centroid inside own polygon: True # TAGORE ANG MO KIO 2 centroid inside own polygon: True # KIAN TECK JURONG WEST 2 centroid inside own polygon: True

Check yourself

Three questions

Clear all three and session 5 gets stamped in your passport.

1 · On the 2026-10-22 snapshot, Kian Teck shows 5,000 cases per 100,000 residents and Taman Jurong next door shows 240.5. What is the right reading?

Both numbers are correct arithmetic from real data. A reads a denominator effect as a finding; C invents a number; D throws away a useful level. The honest move is B: print the residents next to the rate and write the denominator into the header.

2 · The Census 2020 subzone table carries "-" for 46 of the 332 subzones. How should a join handle them?

A zero denominator is a division error at best and a rate of infinity at worst; dropping or filling the rows hides the state of knowledge. Null keeps the subzone on the map with an honest cell.

3 · Why does this page join NEA dengue clusters rather than COVID cases to the subzones?

B is a health judgement this page does not make. C invents a technical reason. D is the opposite of what NEA does: a cluster is a locality built from addresses, with the addresses never published, which is exactly the property that makes it usable.
